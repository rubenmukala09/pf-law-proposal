The 10 most common music industry contracts

We draft, review, and negotiate the agreements that shape music careers, including record, publishing, management, producer, distribution, licensing, and merchandising deals, for artists, labels, producers, managers, and songwriters.

Overview

Contract counsel is essential in the music industry, where a single poorly drafted clause can cost you millions in royalties, ownership rights, or creative freedom. From rising Atlanta artists signing their first record deal to established producers negotiating 360 contracts, every page of every agreement matters. At The Law Offices of Precious Felder, LLC, our team helps artists, labels, producers, managers, and songwriters define the relationships that shape their careers. Below, we break down the 10 most common music industry contracts, what they mean, and why having a skilled entertainment attorney in your corner can protect your income, your copyrights, and your future.

Recording contract (record deal)

A contract between an artist and a record label where the label agrees to finance and distribute the artist’s music in exchange for a percentage of the revenue.

Types:

  • Standard Recording Contract: The label pays for recording, marketing, and distribution and takes a large share of profits.
  • 360 Deal: The label receives a percentage of all of an artist’s income streams (e.g., touring, merchandise, endorsements) in exchange for broader support.

Publishing deal

This contract involves the management of an artist’s songwriting rights. A music publisher ensures songs are used in movies, commercials, or covered by other artists.

Types:

  • Traditional Publishing Deal: The publisher owns a percentage of the copyright and splits royalties with the songwriter.
  • Co-Publishing Deal: The songwriter retains a larger share of the copyright and royalties, but the publisher still has some rights.
  • Administration Deal: The songwriter keeps full ownership, and the publisher administers the rights for a fee or percentage of the income.

Management contract

An agreement between an artist and a manager, where the manager is responsible for guiding the artist’s career and negotiating deals. Managers typically take a percentage (usually 15-20%) of the artist’s income in exchange for their services.

Producer agreement

A contract between an artist (or label) and a music producer, detailing the producer’s role in creating an album or track. This includes payment terms, royalties, and credits. Producers may receive an upfront fee plus points (a percentage of the royalties).

Distribution deal

An agreement where a distribution company agrees to distribute an artist’s music to various platforms (e.g., streaming services, physical stores).

Types:

  • Physical Distribution Deal: Focuses on distributing physical copies like CDs or vinyl.
  • Digital Distribution Deal: Focuses on distributing music through digital platforms.

Licensing agreement

A contract that allows a third party to use a song or recording in various media (e.g., films, commercials) in exchange for a fee. It can involve master recording rights (the actual sound recording) or synchronization rights (use of the composition in timed relation to visual media).

Songwriter agreement

A contract between a songwriter and a publisher or an artist, specifying the terms under which the songs will be used. This can involve terms for royalties, ownership of the songs, and duration of the agreement.

Sync licensing agreement

A type of licensing agreement specifically for the use of music in synchronization with visual media, such as in movies, TV shows, or commercials. The licensee pays a fee to the songwriter or publisher for the right to use the music.

Live performance agreement

A contract between an artist and a venue or promoter for live performances. It includes details on performance fees, rider requirements (e.g., technical needs, hospitality), and revenue sharing for ticket sales.

Merchandising agreement

A contract that allows a company to manufacture and sell merchandise bearing the artist’s name, image, or likeness. The artist typically receives a percentage of the sales. These contracts are essential in defining roles, responsibilities, and financial arrangements within the music industry. Each contract is tailored to the specific needs and goals of the parties involved.
