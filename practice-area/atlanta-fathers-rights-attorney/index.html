Family Law · Legitimation & Fathers’ Rights

Establishing your legal role as a parent

We help unmarried fathers throughout metro Atlanta legitimate their children, secure custody and visitation arrangements, and protect their parental role, including in contested legitimation hearings.

Overview

In Georgia, an unmarried father does not automatically acquire legal parental rights when his child is born, even when his name appears on the birth certificate and he is actively involved in the child’s life. Until a formal legitimation action is completed, critical rights regarding custody, visitation, and decision-making remain with the mother alone. Establishing those rights through the courts is both a legal necessity and a meaningful step in defining your role as a father.

The Law Offices of Precious Felder, LLC helps fathers throughout metro Atlanta legitimate their children, secure appropriate custody and visitation arrangements, and protect their parental role for the long term.

Understanding legitimation under Georgia law

Legitimation is the formal legal process by which a biological father establishes a recognized parent-child relationship with a child born outside of marriage. Once a court grants legitimation, the father is entitled to seek custody and visitation, participate in major decisions about the child’s upbringing, and be considered on equal legal footing with the mother. The controlling statute is O.C.G.A. § 19-7-22.

It is important to distinguish legitimation from paternity. A paternity action establishes biological fatherhood and can support an obligation to pay child support, but it does not, on its own, confer custodial or decision-making rights. Only a legitimation order grants a father the full set of legal rights associated with parentage. Broader information on family court procedures is available through the Judicial Council of Georgia.

What a legitimation action addresses

A successful legitimation action typically results in a court order that confirms the father’s legal relationship to the child and, in most cases, also addresses custody, a structured visitation or parenting-time schedule, and child support calculated under Georgia’s statutory guidelines. Courts evaluate these issues under the best-interests-of-the-child standard, which makes preparation (including documentation of your involvement, support, and commitment) essential to a favorable outcome.

Addressing objections and complications

Legitimation petitions are sometimes contested. When a mother objects, the court must determine whether the father has abandoned his opportunity interest in the child, and must then assess whether legitimation serves the child’s best interests. These hearings require careful evidentiary preparation and experienced advocacy. Our firm handles contested proceedings with the professionalism and thoroughness they demand.

How we help

Our firm provides fathers with focused, strategic representation at every stage of the legitimation process. We begin with a detailed review of your circumstances and objectives, then guide you through filing, negotiation, and, where necessary, a contested hearing. From the initial consultation through entry of a final order, you will work directly with counsel committed to securing your rights and to supporting the lasting relationship you are working to build with your child.
