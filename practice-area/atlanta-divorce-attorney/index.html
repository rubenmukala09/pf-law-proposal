Overview

Divorce is one of the most significant transitions a person can face. It touches nearly every part of your life: your home, your children, your finances, and your sense of what comes next. You deserve counsel that treats those stakes with the seriousness they require, while still making the process understandable from start to finish.

The Law Offices of Precious Felder, LLC represents individuals and families throughout Fulton, DeKalb, Gwinnett, Cobb, and surrounding counties. We take the time to understand your goals, explain each step in plain language, and build a strategy that protects what matters most.

Understanding the divorce process in Georgia

A Georgia divorce begins when one spouse files a Complaint for Divorce in the appropriate superior court. The opposing spouse is then formally served and given an opportunity to respond. From there, the case proceeds through a structured process that typically includes temporary orders, financial disclosures, negotiation or mediation, and, if resolution cannot be reached, a final hearing before the court. The governing statutes are set out in O.C.G.A. Title 19.

Georgia recognizes both no-fault and fault-based grounds for divorce. The most common ground is that the marriage is irretrievably broken, though fault grounds such as adultery, cruel treatment, or desertion remain available and can affect the outcome in certain cases. Parties required to attend parenting seminars can find approved course listings and additional resources through the Judicial Council of Georgia.

Uncontested vs. contested divorce

An uncontested divorce is appropriate when both spouses agree on all material issues: the division of property and debt, any support obligations, and a complete parenting plan where children are involved. These matters typically resolve more quickly and at a lower cost. A contested divorce is required when disagreement exists on one or more issues; in those cases, our firm advocates firmly on your behalf through negotiation, mediation, and, where necessary, litigation.

Key issues resolved in every divorce

Every Georgia divorce addresses four core issues: equitable division of marital assets and debts, spousal support (alimony) where appropriate, legal and physical custody of any minor children, and child support calculated under Georgia’s statutory guidelines. Each issue carries long-term financial and personal consequences, which is why careful preparation, accurate disclosures, and experienced negotiation are essential from the outset.
